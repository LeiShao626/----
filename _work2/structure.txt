  [55,362] 5. Why Curriculum Learning Works
  [55,382] Having established that IS governs the exploration-execution trade-off in trained models (§4.4), we now con-front a fundamental question: how can we train a modelto operate in the low-IS regime required for robust explo-ration? This reveals a paradox inherent to Latent CoT train-ing. Unlike Explicit CoT, which benefits from token-levelsupervision at each reasoning step, Latent CoT must learnto generate meaningful latent representations hk withoutexplicit ground truth.
  [307,426] 5.2. Curriculum Learning as Controlled IS Transition
  [307,445] From the perspective of the Symbolic Index, this canbe understood as a controlled transition. In early stages(pure CoT), high IS ensures accurate token-level gradientsthrough error-correcting discretization. In hybrid stages,the prefix operates in latent space (low IS), but the suffixretains explicit token generation. This anchors the latentrepresentations to valid reasoning traces. Finally, the modelachieves the low IS required for exploration, stabilized bythe progressive internalization.
  [55,503] 5.1. The Training Paradox of Latent CoT
  [55,522] An untrained model has no incentive to perform multi-stepreasoning encoded in hk; instead, it tends to learn ”shortcut”representations that merely map surface-level input patternsto outputs. Training requires reliable gradient signals, whichnecessitate high-certainty (high-IS) predictions to minimizeloss variance. However, the ultimate goal is to learn low-IS representations that enable exploration. Direct trainingthus traps the model in a high-IS regime, preventing theemergence of true latent reasoning.
  [307,559] In our IL framework, this procedure effectively provides su-pervised samples of latent thoughts grounded in correctreasoning, equivalent to drawing samples directly fromPθ∗(h|x).
